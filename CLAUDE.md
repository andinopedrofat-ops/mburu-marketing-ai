## 03.3 — Typography System

**PRIMARY TYPEFACE**: Bold, condensed sans-serif (Bebas Neue, Impact, or equivalent)
- Used for: Headlines, hooks, slide titles, key moments
- Weight: Ultra-bold to black
- Case: ALL CAPS for maximum impact
- Letter-spacing: Tight (−0.02 to −0.05em)

**SECONDARY TYPEFACE**: Clean modern sans-serif (Inter, Helvetica Neue, or equivalent)
- Used for: Body copy, captions, supporting text
- Weight: Regular to medium
- Case: Sentence case for warmth, readability

**ACCENT**: Handwritten or script font (sparingly)
- Used for: Testimonial quotes, member names, human moments
- Maximum 1 instance per piece

## 03.4 — Visual Language Rules

**CINEMATOGRAPHY STANDARDS**
- Every shot has a clear subject and intentional background
- Depth of field: Shallow DOF for close emotion, wide for community/scale
- Movement: Intentional — no shaky handheld unless authenticity is the point
- No static talking heads unless the content is elite-level testimony

**LIGHTING DIRECTIVES**
- Gym scenes: High contrast, dramatic side lighting or overhead LED grid
- Outdoor/lifestyle: Golden hour preferred — warmth, glow, aspiration
- Testimonial/interview: Soft directional light, slight rim light for cinematic depth
- Dark scenes: Deep shadows, a single yellow accent light for brand reinforcement

**COLOR GRADING MOOD BOARDS**

*Power Mode* — High contrast, crushed blacks, boosted yellows. Feels like a championship.
*Transformation Mode* — Desaturated "before" / vivid "after." Visual proof of change.
*Community Mode* — Warm tones, lifted shadows, golden warmth. Feels like belonging.
*Raw Mode* — Minimal grade, slight film grain. Maximum authenticity.

# SECTION 05 — NEUROMARKETING RULES

These principles govern every content decision. They are non-negotiable.

## 05.1 — The Six Persuasion Levers

**01. IDENTITY SHIFT (Primary Lever)**
Never sell the gym. Sell who they become. The brain does not buy products — it buys versions of itself.
> "You're not joining a gym. You're becoming the person who shows up for themselves."
> "This isn't a fitness program. It's the beginning of a different story."

**02. LOSS AVERSION (2.5x more powerful than gain)**
What does staying the same cost them? Make the cost of inaction vivid and real. Not fear-based — truth-based.
> "In 6 months, you'll wish you started today." ← Too cliché.
> "Mauro waited two more years. He still thinks about what he missed." ← Real. Specific. Haunting.

**03. MIRROR EFFECT (Recognition Before Persuasion)**
The audience must see themselves in the content before they will listen to the message. Describe their life, their excuses, their fears with surgical accuracy. They will feel understood. That feeling opens the door.

**04. SOCIAL PROOF (Specificity = Credibility)**
Generic testimonials are worthless. Specific ones are conversion machines.
> Weak: "I love this gym! Great community."
> Strong: "I came in at 94kg, couldn't do a pull-up, and was embarrassed. Now I'm at 82kg and I just did my first unassisted pull-up. The coaches never made me feel like I didn't belong."

**05. MICRO-COMMITMENT LADDER**
Never ask for the full commitment immediately. Build a ladder:
- Step 1: Watch the Reel (done — they're watching)
- Step 2: Save or share the post (low commitment)
- Step 3: DM for information (medium commitment)
- Step 4: Book free class (first real commitment)
- Step 5: Attend free class (physical commitment)
- Step 6: Enroll (full commitment)
Each piece of content advances them one step. Not five.

**06. SCARCITY + TIMING**
Every offer has a deadline. Every class has limited spots. Every cohort has a start date. Urgency must be real — never fabricated. Real urgency converts. Fake urgency destroys trust.

## 05.2 — Dopamine Triggers in Content

Use these structural elements to trigger neurological engagement:
- **Open loops**: Start content with a question or incomplete statement that the brain must resolve. Drives watch-time and swipe-through.
- **Pattern interruption**: Begin with something unexpected — silence, a counter-intuitive statement, an unusual visual.
- **Reward structures**: Deliver the payoff (the answer, the result, the revelation) only after emotional investment is built.
- **Specificity**: Specific numbers, names, timeframes, and details activate credibility centers in the brain.
- **Contrast**: Before/after, was/is, used to/now. The brain is wired for comparison.

## 05.3 — The 3-Second Rule

The human brain makes a content judgment in 3 seconds or less. In that window:
- The hook must land
- The visual quality must signal credibility
- The emotional register must be clear

If any of these three fail in second 1–3, the content fails. Period.

## 05.4 — Color Psychology Application in Neuromarketing

**Deep Blue (#0A1628)**: Activates trust, authority, and calm focus. Used in backgrounds and authority frames to signal that Mburucuya is serious and credible.

**Energetic Yellow (#FFD600)**: Activates attention, energy, and optimism. The brain notices yellow before any other color in a blue field. Used for CTAs, highlights, and key text to drive action.

**High Contrast Blue/Yellow**: Creates visual tension that the brain finds compelling and difficult to ignore. This combination is the brand's visual signature and its most powerful attention tool.

# SECTION 07 — REEL STRUCTURE SYSTEM

## 07.1 — Master Reel Architecture

Every Reel follows this sequence without exception:

```
[0.0s – 2.0s]  ▌ HOOK FRAME
                 The single most important moment.
                 Visual + audio + text must all lock together.
                 No intro, no logo, no context. Just the hook.

[2.0s – 8.0s]  ▌ TENSION BUILD
                 Deepen the problem or the curiosity.
                 Don't resolve anything yet.
                 Layer in emotion. Make them feel something.

[8.0s – 20.0s] ▌ CONTENT CORE
                 Deliver the value, the story, the argument.
                 Scene cuts every 2–3 seconds.
                 Text overlays reinforce audio, not repeat it.

[20.0s – 27.0s]▌ EMOTIONAL PEAK
                 The revelation, the result, the shift.
                 This is the moment they save or share.

[27.0s – 35.0s]▌ RESOLUTION + CTA
                 Land the message. Brand moment.
                 Single, clear call to action.
```

## 07.2 — Reel Production Checklist

**PRE-PRODUCTION**
- [ ] Hook is defined BEFORE shooting begins
- [ ] Emotional arc mapped (3 acts minimum)
- [ ] Shot list created with timing
- [ ] Music track selected and tempo noted
- [ ] Brand color placement planned

**PRODUCTION**
- [ ] First 2 seconds shot multiple ways for A/B testing
- [ ] Vertical format (9:16) throughout
- [ ] Audio quality confirmed (gym noise can be used as texture, not noise)
- [ ] Minimum 3 angles per scene
- [ ] Real member/coach captured, not actor

**POST-PRODUCTION**
- [ ] Hook text overlay in yellow or white, ultra-bold, center frame
- [ ] Brand color grade applied (deep blue/yellow palette reinforced)
- [ ] Music peaks align with visual cuts
- [ ] Subtitles/captions on (80% of Reels watched muted)
- [ ] Logo frame at end (Deep Blue background, white logo, yellow CTA text)
- [ ] Duration: 15–35 seconds for discovery, 35–60s for depth content

## 07.3 — Reel Types & Templates

**TYPE A — THE MIRROR REEL**
Opens with a statement that describes exactly how the target customer feels. They see themselves immediately. Resolution shows transformation through Mburucuya.

**TYPE B — THE PROOF REEL**
Opens on a real member or real result. Timeline specificity. Raw and cinematic simultaneously. Ends with their direct invitation to the viewer.

**TYPE C — THE MYTH-BREAKER REEL**
Opens with a common fitness belief stated confidently. Immediately subverts it with evidence or logic. Builds authority, drives saves and shares.

**TYPE D — THE ATMOSPHERE REEL**
No voiceover. Pure cinematic gym footage. Music-driven. Designed to trigger aspiration and FOMO. Works for cold audiences who don't know the brand.

**TYPE E — THE COACH MANIFESTO**
Coach speaks directly to camera. Unscripted, 1–2 takes. Raw, honest, specific. No polish. Maximum authenticity. These perform best when the message is counter-intuitive.

- **Brand color presence**: At least one Deep Blue or Yellow element per slide
- **Image/icon usage**: One per slide maximum — supporting, never competing with text
- **Logo**: Bottom right corner, small, every slide except the hook (where it appears at full prominence)

# SECTION 09 — HOOKS LIBRARY

## 09.1 — Hook Categories and Examples

**CATEGORY A — COUNTER-INTUITIVE STATEMENTS**
These challenge a common belief. The brain cannot scroll past something that contradicts what it thinks is true.

- "El gym no te va a cambiar. Vos sí."
- "Entrenar más no es la respuesta."
- "La gente que está en forma no tiene más fuerza de voluntad que vos."
- "Lo que te falta no es motivación."
- "El peor momento para empezar el gym es cuando estás listo."
- "No vengas a Mburucuya si buscás resultados rápidos."

**CATEGORY B — DIRECT CONFRONTATION HOOKS**
Name the exact pain the audience feels. Make them stop because they feel recognized.

- "Empezaste mil veces y paraste."
- "Te cansas subiendo escaleras y lo odias."
- "Hace cuánto que prometiste empezar el lunes?"
- "Te molesta lo que ves en el espejo. Y lo ignorás."
- "Tenés ropa que no te cierra y no la tirás porque 'ya vas a volver'."

**CATEGORY C — VISUAL PATTERN INTERRUPTION HOOKS**
The visual itself is the hook. The text is secondary.

- Extreme close-up on chalk-covered hands gripping a bar. Text: "Acá empieza todo."
- Black screen. Yellow text fades in: "Hoy." Then cut.
- Slow motion chalk cloud explosion. No text. Music cuts in.
- Member's face at peak effort. Text: "Esto es lo que se siente hacer algo difícil."

**CATEGORY D — OPEN LOOP HOOKS**
Leave a question open that the brain must resolve.

- "Le dijeron que nunca iba a poder. Lo primero que hizo fue mostrarles que estaban equivocados."
- "Hace 6 meses, Valeria no podía subir un tramo de escaleras. Hoy:"
- "Hay algo que nadie te dice sobre empezar en el gym."
- "Esto que estás a punto de ver nos pidieron que no lo publicáramos."

**CATEGORY E — IDENTITY ACTIVATION HOOKS**
Challenge or affirm who they are or could be.

- "¿Sos del tipo de persona que arranca o del tipo que espera?"
- "La versión de vos que querés ser ya sabe a dónde tiene que ir."
- "Hay gente que dice 'algún día'. Y hay gente que dice 'hoy'."

**CATEGORY F — CINEMATIC SCENE HOOKS**
Opens with a visual scene instead of a statement. Creates atmospheric immersion.

- "5:30am. El gym está vacío. Excepto por ella."
- "Primer día. Estuvo parado en la puerta 4 minutos antes de entrar."
- "La barra pesa 80kg. Hace 3 meses no podía levantar 30."

## 09.2 — Hook Construction Rules

1. **No more than 8 words** for text-based hooks. Brain processes short bursts under pressure.
2. **Lead with the tension**, not the resolution. The hook is never the answer.
3. **One clear subject** — the hook names one person, one pain, or one idea. Never two.
4. **Activate one emotion** per hook — recognition, curiosity, or challenge. Never mix.
5. **A/B test every hook** — shoot 2–3 variations of the opening 2 seconds for every Reel.

# SECTION 10 — CTA RULES SYSTEM

## 10.1 — The Single CTA Law

Every piece of content ends with exactly **ONE call to action**. Never two. The brain presented with two options often chooses neither. Choose the right action for where this audience is in the funnel.

## 10.2 — CTA by Funnel Stage

**TOP OF FUNNEL (Cold Audience — Discovery)**
Audience: Doesn't know Mburucuya yet. Found through algorithm.
Goal: One micro-commitment. Low friction.
> "Guardá este post si esto te resuena."
> "Seguinos para más."
> "Mandanos un DM con 'INFO' y te contamos todo."

**MIDDLE OF FUNNEL (Warm Audience — Consideration)**
Audience: Has seen content before. Follows or has engaged.
Goal: Move toward a conversation or free experience.
> "Tu primera clase es gratis. Link en bio para reservar."
> "Escribinos 'QUIERO EMPEZAR' y te armamos un plan."
> "Reservá tu lugar para la semana de prueba gratuita."

**BOTTOM OF FUNNEL (Hot Audience — Decision)**
Audience: Has engaged multiple times, may have sent a DM, is clearly interested.
Goal: Enrollment or direct conversion.
> "Quedan [X] lugares para el turno de las 7am. Reservá hoy."
> "La inscripción cierra el viernes. Este es tu último aviso."
> "Llamanos ahora. El cambio empieza cuando vos decidís."

## 10.3 — CTA Design Rules

**Visual Hierarchy**: CTA text always in Energetic Yellow on Deep Blue, or Deep Blue on Yellow. Never low contrast.

**Urgency Language**: Real urgency only. Specify actual numbers, actual dates.
> Wrong: "¡No te lo pierdas!"
> Right: "Quedan 4 lugares para el turno de las 6am. Cierra el jueves."

**Friction Audit**: For every CTA, ask "What is the minimum number of steps for someone to complete this action?" Reduce by at least one step.

**WhatsApp CTAs**: Always include the phone number. Never just say "escribinos" without a direct link or number.

# SECTION 11 — STORYTELLING RULES

## 11.1 — The Mburucuya Story Architecture

Every story told through Mburucuya content follows the same structural DNA:

```
ACT I — THE BEFORE (Empathy)
Context: Who was this person? What was life like?
Emotion target: Recognition, validation, "that's me"
Rules: Be specific. Name the weight, the excuse, the moment.

ACT II — THE MOMENT (Tension → Decision)
Context: What happened? What made them decide?
Emotion target: Hope, possibility, tension
Rules: The decision moment must feel real, not manufactured.
       It's never "I decided to get fit." It's a specific moment.
       "My daughter couldn't keep up with me on a walk. That was it."

ACT III — THE PROCESS (Reality)
Context: What did the journey actually look like?
Emotion target: Respect, belief, authenticity
Rules: Include struggle. Include a moment of doubt.
       Without friction, the result is meaningless.

ACT IV — THE AFTER (Proof)
Context: Where are they now? What specifically changed?
Emotion target: Aspiration, inspiration, decision activation
Rules: Specificity is everything.
       Not "I feel better." → "I sleep 7 hours. My back doesn't hurt. I ran 5km last month."

ACT V — THE INVITATION (Bridge)
Context: What would they say to someone like them?
Emotion target: Belonging, possibility, urgency
Rules: The invitation must come from the member, not the brand.
       Let them speak to their past self — and to your viewer.
```

## 11.2 — Storytelling Principles

**SPECIFICITY OVER INSPIRATION**
Specific details create credibility. Inspiration without specificity is noise.
> Weak: "She lost a lot of weight and feels amazing."
> Strong: "Florencia lost 14kg over 5 months. She's 38. She'd never lifted weights before. Now she deadlifts 60kg and says it's the most confident she's felt in her adult life."

**THE EARNED EMOTION RULE**
Never manufacture emotion. Let the story earn it. If you have to tell the audience to feel something, the story hasn't done its job.

**SHOW, DON'T STATE**
> Never: "This community is incredible."
> Always: "When Diego's father passed away, the coaches showed up to his house. Not because they had to. Because that's who we are."

## 13.2 — Viral Content Audit Checklist

Before any content is published, it must pass this checklist:

**THE HOOK TEST**
- [ ] Does the first 2 seconds create genuine curiosity or recognition?
- [ ] Would you stop scrolling for this if you didn't know the brand?
- [ ] Is there a pattern interruption in the first frame?

**THE EMOTION TEST**
- [ ] Does this content make you feel something specific?
- [ ] Can you name the exact emotion it targets?
- [ ] Is there an emotional arc (not just information delivery)?

**THE SHARE TEST**
- [ ] Why would someone share this? What does sharing it say about them?
- [ ] Is there a "this is so me" moment?
- [ ] Is there a "my friend needs to see this" moment?

**THE SAVE TEST**
- [ ] Does this contain information or inspiration worth returning to?
- [ ] Is the value dense enough to earn a save?

**THE BRAND TEST**
- [ ] Is this unmistakably Mburucuya (not generic fitness content)?
- [ ] Are brand colors present in at least 30% of the visual time?
- [ ] Does the voice sound like Mburucuya?

# SECTION 14 — COPYWRITING SYSTEM

## 14.1 — Caption Architecture

Every Instagram caption follows this structure:

```
LINE 1:    The hook. Single sentence. Must make them tap "more."
           Max 125 characters (what shows before "more" cut-off).

LINE 2–3:  Empty line. Then open the emotional payload.
           First person. Real voice. 2–4 sentences.

MIDDLE:    The body. Story or value delivery. Short paragraphs.
           Never more than 2 sentences per paragraph.
           One line break between each paragraph.

PENULTIMATE: The pivot. Bridge from content to action.
             "Si esto te resuena..." / "El próximo paso es tuyo."

LAST LINE: The CTA. Bold. Single. Clear.

HASHTAGS:  First comment (preferred) or end of caption, 5–10 tags max.
           Mix: Brand-specific + local + community + content-type.
```

## 14.2 — Hashtag Architecture

**TIER 1 — Brand Tags** (always include)
`#MburucuyaFitness #GimnasioMburucuya #MburucuyaCenter`

**TIER 2 — Community Tags** (rotate)

**TIER 5 — Discovery Tags** (broader reach)

**Maximum**: 10 tags per post. Quality over quantity.

## 14.3 — WhatsApp Copywriting System

**THE PERSONAL MESSAGE FRAMEWORK**

Every WhatsApp message from Mburucuya must feel like it was written specifically for that one person. Structure:

```
OPENER (1 sentence):
  Address them by name. Reference their specific context.
  "Hola Marcos, te contacto porque nos escribiste por las clases de las mañanas."

ACKNOWLEDGMENT (1–2 sentences):
  Validate what they're looking for. Mirror their language.
  "Entiendo que estás buscando algo que se adapte a tu horario y que te de resultados reales."

BRIDGE (1–2 sentences):
  Connect their need to the Mburucuya solution.
  "Justamente el turno de las 7am está armado para eso..."

PROOF (1 sentence):
  Specific, real social proof related to their profile.
  "La semana pasada, un profe con el mismo horario que vos arrancó y ya está viniendo 3 veces por semana."

OFFER (1 sentence):
  The micro-commitment ask. Low friction.
  "¿Querés que te reservamos un lugar para una clase de prueba gratis esta semana?"

CTA (1 line):
  Clear. Human. Not salesy.
  "Decime qué día te queda mejor y lo coordinamos."
```

**WhatsApp Tone Rules**:
- Always first person ("yo", "nosotros")
- Use contractions as native speakers do ("querés" not "quieres")
- Never use bullet points — conversational paragraphs only
- One emoji maximum per message, and only if it feels natural
- Response time: Under 2 hours. After 4 hours: acknowledge the wait before continuing.

## 14.4 — Power Words by Emotional Target

**For IDENTITY activation**:
forjado, versión, convertirte, decidir, elegir, construir, merecés, valés

**For URGENCY**:
hoy, ahora, últimos, cierra, quedan, esta semana, no esperes, ya

**For EMPATHY**:
entiendo, sé que, es normal, no sos el único, también, reconozco

**For AUTHORITY**:
probado, real, resultados, específico, método, sistema, comunidad

**For HOPE**:
posible, podés, empezás, primero, cambio, diferente, esta vez

## 14.5 — Anti-Copywriting Rules (What to Never Write)

- Never use exclamation marks excessively (max 1 per caption)
- Never write "¡Hola!" as an opener in any context
- Never use "Para más información, visita..." — always make it personal
- Never use passive voice when active voice is possible
- Never write more than 3 sentences without a line break
- Never end with a question AND a CTA — choose one
- Never use "increíble" or "fantástico" — be specific instead

# SECTION 19 — BEHAVIORAL OPERATING RULES

These rules govern every output of the Mburucuya Marketing Intelligence System.

1. **Every output is Mburucuya-specific.** Nothing generic, nothing that could belong to any other gym.

2. **Lead with emotion, close with action.** Never open with the offer. Always earn the audience's trust first.

3. **Specificity is credibility.** Names, weights, dates, timeframes, specific struggles. Generic kills conversion.

4. **No stock-photo thinking.** Every visual idea is shot-on-location, with real people, in the actual gym.

5. **The hook is 80% of everything.** If asked to refine one element, refine the hook first, last, and always.

6. **One CTA per piece.** No exceptions.

7. **The brand colors are load-bearing.** Deep Blue and Energetic Yellow must appear in every piece. They are not decorative — they are psychological tools.

8. **WhatsApp is human, always.** Every script is written as a real staff member, not a system. First person. Never robotic.

9. **Never shame the audience.** Not their body, not their past failures, not their fears. Empathy first. Always.

10. **The community is the product.** Mburucuya is not selling workouts — it's selling belonging, transformation, and identity. The gym is the mechanism. The community is the reason.
